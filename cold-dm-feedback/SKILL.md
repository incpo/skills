# Cold DM Feedback Writer

Write short, honest cold DMs that get real feedback from real users — not polite lies.

The whole game: people lie to be nice. Good feedback questions make lying harder than telling the truth. The DM is the wrapper around questions that pass the Mom Test.

## Core Principles

### 1. The Mom Test (Rob Fitzpatrick)
The Mom Test says: ask questions so specific and behavior-grounded that even your mom can't lie to you.

**Never ask:**
- "Would you use this?" → hypothetical = fantasy
- "Do you like it?" → fishing for compliments
- "Is this a good idea?" → opinion on idea = useless
- "Would you pay for it?" → easy to lie

**Always ask:**
- "When did you last do X?" → past behavior, can't lie
- "How are you solving this today?" → real workflow
- "What's the worst part of doing X?" → real pain
- "Have you ever paid for a solution to X?" → real spend
- "Walk me through the last time you...?" → story = truth

### 2. YC "How to Talk to Users" (Eric Migicovsky, Gustaf Alströmer)
- Avoid yes/no questions — they give you no signal
- Focus on problems, not features — users describe bad problems poorly, but worse: they describe bad solutions confidently
- Don't pitch your idea hard — the goal is data extraction, not selling
- Open questions > closed questions

### 3. Paul Graham — Sound Human
- AI-written DMs feel like being lied to. People stop reading.
- Short. No marketing-speak. No "we shipped" + "delight" + "elevate" combos.
- Write like a friend who's working on something and wants honest input.
- If a teenager could produce it with one prompt, it's not good enough.

### 4. Respect Time
- ~80 words, max ~120
- One link. One demo. Three questions tops.
- Make the cost of replying tiny.

## Anatomy of a Good Cold DM

Five parts, in this order:

```
1. Personal hook        → why THEM (one line, signal-based)
2. Frame                → "testing an idea" or "running something past you"
                          (NOT "we shipped!" — sounds like marketing)
3. What it does         → one plain sentence. No hype words.
4. Link                 → one clean URL, prefixed with → or "demo:"
5. Behavior questions   → 2–3 max, Mom Test approved
6. Permission to say no → reduces social pressure to lie
```

Skip part 6 only if the DM is already very short.

## Question Patterns That Work

Pick 2–3 per DM. Mix at least one behavior question with at most one feature-feedback question.

**Behavior (Mom Test gold):**
- "When did you last [problem activity]? How did you do it?"
- "What's the worst part of [activity] for you?"
- "How do you [current workflow] today?"
- "Walk me through the last time you needed to [X]."
- "How often does [problem] come up for you?"

**Light feature feedback (only after they've seen the demo):**
- "After the demo — anything you'd change or add?"
- "Anything that confused you?"
- "What's the first thing you'd want it to do that it doesn't?"

**Permission-to-bail closer:**
- "Even 'I never do this' tells us a lot."
- "Honest 'not for me' is just as useful."
- "No pressure if it's not your thing."

## Banned Phrases

These sound like marketing emails or AI:

- ❌ "We're excited to announce..."
- ❌ "We just shipped a game-changing..."
- ❌ "Would love your thoughts!"
- ❌ "Hope this finds you well"
- ❌ "Helps you save time and money"
- ❌ "Cuts X down to seconds" (only OK if specific and earned)
- ❌ "Empowers you to..."
- ❌ "Revolutionary / next-gen / cutting-edge"
- ❌ "What do you think?" (vague, low response)
- ❌ Closing with just a generic question to the reader

If a phrase could appear in any product launch email, cut it.

## Tone Calibration

- Friendly, not formal. Like texting a smart friend.
- Confident, not pushy. State the thing simply, ask honestly.
- Curious, not defensive. You want to learn, not sell.
- First-person plural ("we") when speaking for a team/company.
- First-person singular ("I") when it's a solo or it's personally yours.

Match the platform:
- **Discord / Telegram / DMs:** very casual, fragments OK, lowercase first word OK
- **Email:** slightly more structured, full sentences, subject line included
- **X / Twitter DM:** shortest possible — 3–5 sentences

## Length Guide

| Platform | Target words | Questions |
|---|---|---|
| X DM | 40–60 | 1–2 |
| Discord/Telegram DM | 70–100 | 2–3 |
| Cold email | 80–120 | 2–3 |

Never exceed 120 words for cold outreach. Long DMs feel like work.

## Examples

### Example 1 — Discord DM, English (good)

**Context:** Founder of acme.app asking active user about a financial report diff tool.

```
Hey [name]!

You're an active user on acme.app — testing an idea and wanted your take.

We built a tool that compares two financial reports side by side and highlights every change between them.

Quick demo on Apple → acme.app/quote/aapl/diff

A few honest questions:

→ when did you last compare two filings? how did you do it?
→ what's the worst part about it for you?
→ after the demo — anything you'd change or add?

Even "I never compare reports" tells us a lot.
```

Why it works:
- Personal hook ("active user on acme.app") = signal-based
- "Testing an idea" frame = honest, not marketing
- One plain sentence describing the tool
- One link, one anchor company
- Mix: 2 behavior questions + 1 feature-feedback question
- Permission to bail at the end

### Example 2 — same context, bad (what NOT to write)

```
Hi there! 👋

We're SUPER excited to announce that we just shipped a game-changing
feature on acme.app that will revolutionize how you analyze financial
reports! It cuts hours of work down to seconds and helps you stay
ahead of the market.

Check it out: acme.app/quote/aapl/diff

Would you use it? Do you think it's useful? What do you think??

Let us know!! 🚀🚀
```

Why it fails:
- Marketing-speak from word one
- "Would you use it?" = hypothetical lie magnet
- "Do you think it's useful?" = fishing for compliments
- Two emojis = AI tells
- Zero personal hook
- No permission to say no
- ~70 words but 0 useful signal

### Example 3 — Telegram DM, Russian

```
Привет!

Ты активный юзер acme.app — тестируем одну штуку, хочется услышать твой честный взгляд.

Сделали тулзу: сравнивает два финансовых отчета и подсвечивает все изменения между ними.

Демо на Apple → acme.app/quote/aapl/diff

Пара вопросов:

→ когда последний раз сравнивал два отчета? как это делал?
→ что самое бесящее в этом процессе?
→ после демо — что бы поменял или добавил?

Даже «я этим вообще не занимаюсь» — полезный ответ.
```

### Example 4 — X DM, English (shortest format)

```
Hey [name] — active acme.app user, wanted your honest take.

Built a thing that diffs two financial reports → acme.app/quote/aapl/diff

When did you last compare two filings, and how'd you do it? What was the worst part?

"Never do this" is a fine answer too.
```

~55 words, one link, two questions, escape valve included.

### Example 5 — Cold email, English

```
Subject: testing a feature — quick honest take?

Hey [name],

Saw you're an active user on acme.app — we're testing something and your view would help a lot.

It compares two financial reports side by side and highlights every change. Demo on Apple: acme.app/quote/aapl/diff

If you have 30 seconds:

→ when did you last compare two filings? how did you do it?
→ what's the most painful part of it for you?
→ after seeing the demo — anything missing or off?

No pressure if it's not your workflow — "doesn't apply to me" is useful info too.

Thanks,
[your name]
```

## Workflow

When asked to write a cold DM:

1. **Get context** if missing — ask only what you can't infer:
   - Platform (Discord / Telegram / X / email)
   - Language
   - What the feature/product is + link
   - Who the target is (active users? cold list? specific persona?)
   - What signal makes them THE recipient (active usage? past purchase? topic they posted about?)

2. **Draft once** following the anatomy.

3. **Self-check against the checklist** before showing the user.

4. **Show only the DM** — don't pre-explain. Let the user read it cold.

5. **Iterate based on feedback.** Common asks:
   - "Make it shorter" → cut adjectives, then a question, then the closer
   - "Less salesy" → strip value-prop sentence, just say what it does
   - "Friendlier" → add 1 personal touch, drop formality, keep questions
   - "Remove cringy words" → kill anything from the banned phrases list

## Pre-Send Checklist

Before delivering the DM, run through this list:

- [ ] Under 120 words (under 100 ideal)
- [ ] One personal hook tied to a real signal about the recipient
- [ ] Frame is "testing / running past you," not "we shipped / launched"
- [ ] One plain sentence describing what it does — no marketing words
- [ ] One link, formatted clean (`→ url` or `demo: url`)
- [ ] 2–3 questions max
- [ ] At least one question is about past behavior (Mom Test approved)
- [ ] Zero "would you use it?" / "is it useful?" / "do you like it?"
- [ ] Permission to say no included (unless DM is already extremely short)
- [ ] Zero banned phrases from the list above
- [ ] Sounds like a human wrote it — not an AI, not a marketer
- [ ] Could a teenager produce this with one prompt? If yes → rewrite
