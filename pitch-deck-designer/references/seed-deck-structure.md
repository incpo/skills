# Seed Deck Structure — Slide-by-Slide Guide

This reference defines the narrative arc and content for each slide in a seed round pitch deck. Adapt the number of slides per section based on complexity, but try to keep each set to n ≤ 3 slides.

---

## Slide 1: Title

**Purpose:** Company name + one-line description of what you do.

- This is the ONLY section that must be exactly 1 slide
- The one-liner should be concrete: "X for Y" or "We do X by doing Y"
- Bad: "Reimagining enterprise collaboration" → Good: "Slack for construction teams"
- Include the company logo if you have one, but keep it clean

**Design:** Can be a bold dark slide. Company name large and centered, one-liner below in slightly smaller but still very legible text.

---

## Slide 2: Problem

**Purpose:** Clearly state the problem you solve. Show real-world impact.

- Make the problem feel REAL — use a specific person, scenario, or data point
- "X people suffer from Y" is stronger than "Y is a problem"
- Don't describe the solution here — just the pain
- If the problem isn't obviously painful, you have a positioning issue, not a slide issue

**Design:** Keep it to 1-2 sentences max. Big text. Let the problem hit hard.

---

## Slide 3: Solution

**Purpose:** Explain what you do in as few words as possible.

- Describe CONCRETE BENEFITS, not features
- Bad: "AI-powered NLP pipeline for document processing"
- Good: "Reads legal contracts in seconds and flags risky clauses"
- The audience should immediately understand why this solves the problem from the previous slide
- If you can't explain it simply, you don't understand it well enough

**Design:** Short, punchy text. If you want to show the product, use 2-3 bullet steps of the user flow, NOT a screenshot.

---

## Slides 4-5: Traction

**Purpose:** Prove it's working. Show the numbers.

- Revenue is best. Users and growth rate are good. Engagement metrics are okay.
- Make the numbers CLEAR and MEANINGFUL — don't just show a graph, state the conclusion explicitly
- "5x revenue growth in 6 months" written ON the slide next to the chart
- If the curve isn't smooth, that's fine — don't fake it
- Second traction slide (optional): additional metrics, notable customers, retention data

**Design:**
- Chart + explicit caption stating the takeaway
- Or large stat callouts: big number (48-72pt) with small label below
- Context stats can go beside the chart if you have impressive supporting numbers

---

## Slide 6: Key Insights / Why You Win

**Purpose:** What makes you special? What insight do you have that others don't?

- This is your "secret" — the non-obvious thing that makes this work
- Could be a technology advantage, unique data, market insight, or distribution edge
- This might take more than one slide if you have multiple strong insights
- Avoid generic claims ("best team" / "first mover") — be specific

**Design:** 2-3 bold bullet points with brief supporting text each. Or a single powerful statement.

---

## Slide 7: Business Model

**Purpose:** How do you make money?

- You probably don't know every detail yet — that's fine
- But you should know: who pays, how much, and how often
- Lay it out simply: "SaaS / $X per month per user" or "Marketplace / 15% take rate"
- If it's complex, use additional slides — but keep each one focused on one aspect

**Design:** Clean layout. Can use a simple table or large text callouts for key numbers (price point, margins, LTV).

---

## Slide 8: Market Size

**Purpose:** Convince the investor this can be BIG.

- Show TAM/SAM/SOM if you have credible numbers
- Better: show a bottoms-up calculation ("X potential customers × $Y per year = $Z")
- Even better: show that the market is GROWING and explain why
- Don't just throw out a huge number from a Gartner report — investors see through that

**Design:** Large market size number prominently displayed with brief supporting logic. Can use a simple funnel or breakdown.

---

## Slide 9: Team

**Purpose:** Show why YOUR team is uniquely suited to solve this problem.

- Focus on FOUNDERS only — nobody cares about advisors at seed stage
- Highlight relevant experience, domain expertise, or unfair advantages
- "Built payments infrastructure at Stripe" matters more than "MBA from Harvard"
- If you have notable achievements (previous exits, deep technical expertise), feature them

**Design:** Photo + name + one-line credential per founder. Keep it clean. 2-3 people max on one slide.

---

## Slide 10: The Ask

**Purpose:** Tell the investor exactly what you need and what it gets them.

- State the raise amount clearly
- Explain what the money funds: "With $X, we will achieve Y by Z date"
- If you can map milestones to Series A readiness, that's powerful
- Be specific: "Hire 3 engineers, launch in 2 new markets, reach $X MRR"

**Design:** Large raise amount, then 3-4 milestone bullets. Clean and confident.

---

## Adaptation for Demo Day (2.5 Minutes)

For a Demo Day presentation, compress to 5-7 slides total:

1. **Title** — company name + one-liner
2. **Problem** — the pain, stated sharply
3. **Solution** — what you do, concretely
4. **Traction** — your best number, made explicit
5. **Market / Insight** — why this is big or why you win (pick the stronger story)
6. **Team** — (optional, can be covered verbally)
7. **Ask** — (optional for Demo Day, often handled in follow-up)

Remember: investors see 100+ companies in a day. They will check email if they don't immediately get your point. Every slide must be instantly understandable.

---

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. **Packing nuances into one slide.** Save nuance for the conversation. Slides are billboards, not essays.
2. **Using screenshots to show the product.** Screenshots are illegible, complex, and non-obvious. Use a simple step list instead.
3. **Leaving graphs unlabeled.** Never show a chart without an explicit caption stating the conclusion.
4. **Small text at the bottom of slides.** The most important text often ends up in the smallest font at the worst position. Fix this.
5. **Too many slides.** More slides ≠ more impressive. Fewer, clearer slides = more memorable.
6. **Diagrams to explain how it works.** Diagrams are mazes. Use words instead.
7. **Generic market claims.** "The global X market is $Y billion" without bottoms-up logic is meaningless.
8. **Advisor slides.** Nobody cares. Cut it.
9. **Competitive matrices.** Rarely helpful. If you must, keep it to a simple 2×2 or short comparison.
10. **Videos or screencasts in slides.** Think twice. Then think a third time. Then probably don't.
